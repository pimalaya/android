# Mail sync robustness: dead connections, failed bodies, the gaps of Load more

Capabilities moved: mail (added: a dead connection is dropped, never read again; a body that fails is tried again in the run; junk and trash list when opened; modified: an account's frontier is where its steps reached, an account's mail comes in steps, the mail list ends on Load more, arrivals download whatever their date, bodies download behind the list). Device test pending, so the change stays active.

**Why.** On Microsoft 365, messages inside the frontier stayed dimmed for good: Graph closes an idle keep-alive connection after 115 to 130 s, `Transport` never dropped a socket, and `PimalayaClient.on()` reopened a session only on a JNI throw, which the bridge never does. A 30 s read timeout could leave an answer on the socket for the next request to read as its own. A failed body was dropped silently for the run, a token expiring mid-run cleared the account's plan, and mail-load-more left three gaps open (junk and trash on demand, a one-mailbox step moving the account's frontier, arrivals kept in memory only).

**Connections.** `Transport` drops an origin's connection on any `IOException` (timeouts included) and at EOF, counting the losses (`losses`), and gains three upcalls: `begin(url)` drops a connection whose last answer was not settled and answers whether the request reuses one, `settle(url)` marks an answer read to its end, `reset(url)` drops it. rust/src/client.rs calls `reset` on any read or write failure, IMAP and SMTP included, and binds the three (`Client::begin`, `settle`, `reset`). throttle.rs begins every new request (`Wire::wire_begin`), follows each answer by its framing (`Follow`: `Content-Length`, a streamed chunked parser `Chunks`, 204 and 304 empty) and settles it at its end; no framing, a cut answer, `Connection: close`, an interim answer or bytes past the end are never settled, so the next request goes on a new connection. A reused connection that ends or fails before the first byte of an answer to GET, HEAD, OPTIONS, TRACE, PUT, DELETE, PROPFIND or REPORT is reset and the recorded request written again once (`resendable`, `resend`); a write failing on one is sent again the same way. `PimalayaClient.on()` reopens and reruns a verb whose reply is an error when the transport lost a connection since a verb last answered (`MailSession.lostSinceAnswered`, `answered`), so an IMAP socket lost under a copy or an append is reopened by the next verb too, and a refusal from a live server is no longer run twice.

**Failed bodies.** `MailEngine.download` answers the rows asked of the server and not filed (`asked`, `answered`), and settles the owed ones that landed. `MailBodies.step` puts a failed row back at the end of its account's plan, at most `ATTEMPTS` (3) a run, then marks it in `FAILED` (process-lived), whose row says *Download failed* (`mail_row_failed`, en and fr); a download failing as a whole puts its chunk back, and a second whole failure in a row takes the account out of the run (`Run.broken`). `MainActivity.downloadBodies` gathers each job's failures, a failed job giving back its rows, closes the account's pool after any so the retry runs on reopened sessions (an expired token renewed by `openMail`), and throws only when every job failed. `GmailStep` owes a step's body that did not land.

**Gaps.** `MailOwed` (shared preferences `mail-owed`, by collection and link id) replaces the process-lived `MailBodies.ARRIVALS`, keyed by link id rather than handle: a delta's new members and a step's failed bodies are owed across restarts; `planBodies` plans the owed ones below their frontier and drops those in junk, trash, gone or already downloaded. `MailWindow` keeps a mailbox's own frontier (preferences `mail-window-mailbox`): `stepped` moves it for a step over one mailbox alone (all mail once exhausted) and the account's otherwise, `since(context, accountId, collection)` reads the older of the two, `more(context, accountId, collection)` drives the footer of a mailbox shown alone, `dropMailboxes` forgets the older ones when the account's moves later and with the account. `MailStore.floorOf`, `mailboxWindow`, the body planner (`windowedRows`), `MailEngine.download`, the row's state, `MessageView` and `WidenStep` read the mailbox's frontier. `MailList` hands the junk and trash it shows alone to `MainActivity.listAlone`, which lists each once per opening on Wi-Fi: a Gmail one by a step over its label (the bridge already sends `includeSpamTrash` for SPAM and TRASH), any other by `MailEngine.sync`.

**Edits outside the generic layer.** rust/src/client.rs and rust/src/client/throttle.rs (shared by every backend); `WidenStep`, one line reading the mailbox's frontier when it steps one alone. No IMAP, Graph or JMAP module, no `MailStep.of`, no `fetch_sources` or `mail_step` dispatch was touched.

**Tests.** Rust (402 green): throttle.rs resend after an end or a reset on a reused connection, none for a POST, a fresh connection or twice; settling at a `Content-Length` end, at a chunked body's end across reads, never for `Connection: close` or no framing, a reset on bytes past the end; a throttled answer given up on settled. Java app (438 green): `MailBodiesTest` (a body refused once read again after the others, one failing every attempt marked failed then cleared by a later run, a whole failure put back and two in a row leaving the run, a delta's arrival owed through the engine's own listing until it lands), `MailWindowTest` (a one-mailbox step's frontier, read, dropped, forgotten), `MailStepTest` (a step's body that did not land owed in each of its mailboxes). Java client (8 green): `TransportTest` against a local server (answering once then closing, resetting, an unsettled answer dropped, `reset`), `PimalayaClientTest` (a failed reply told by its error field).

**Left open.** The device test. A POST lost on a dead connection is not sent again by the transport (the verb's reopening covers it). The failed mark lasts the process.
