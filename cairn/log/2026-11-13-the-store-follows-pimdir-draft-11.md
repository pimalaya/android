# The store follows pimdir draft-11

The app follows pimdir storage-draft-11, sync-draft-11 and search-draft-11 (d8800fb, the fourteen lines draft-10 parked for the standard's owner) on io-pimdir cd33f0e, pinned by a git patch since the crate still reads 0.11.0 and is not released. A store an earlier build wrote, a draft-10 one among them, is refused (`store-version`, or `sync-version` for its sync state) and the app opens on its reinstall screen; an index of the earlier draft is rebuilt.

- **The draft names.** They are the crate's (`sql::FORMAT_VERSION`, `SYNC_VERSION`, `FORMAT_DRAFT`) over the bridge, so the store is created, checked and stamped at draft-11 by the pin alone. The app's own copies moved: the fork of `init_index_meta` (`search-draft-11`), the conformance string, the fixtures.
- **Statements.** The canonical text moved under three names the app runs: `init_store_meta` and `park_action`, which it runs as they are, and `init_index_meta`, whose fork was read again and names the new digest (cc4f09ed8609fc97), only the draft's name having moved. `unconfirm_action` is new and needs a fork (`RETURNING`): a read of the row then its update under the same predicate, in one transaction, as `retry_action` is (digest 42a22ef323409afa). Every other fork still names the digest of the text it was forked from, which `every_fork_names_the_canonical_text_it_rewrites` holds: none of those texts moved.
- **Queue.** The reserved prefix is the statements'. `PimdirQueue.unconfirm` runs `unconfirm_action` and answers whether a row was parked, where it wrote `unconfirmed: ` itself before `park_action`; `PimdirQueue.park` hands its reason over whole, where it wrote `failed: ` itself. The Java that prefixed is gone; the prefix is still read, off the bridge, to tell *May have been sent* from a refusal and to anchor an unconfirmed row again on the outbox. The earlier Java on the new `park_action` would have recorded every unknown outcome as `failed: unconfirmed: `, a refusal.
- **A message's date.** `mail_summary.date` has a shape `CHECK`. Every mail summary Java binds crosses the bridge first (an engine write, a listing, a derivation), so the shape is held where a summary is serialised for Java (rust/src/offline/summary.rs), by io-pimdir's `summary::mail::usable_date`: a date of another shape crosses as none, as `PimdirSummary::stored` writes it. No Java restates the rule. The connectors already state the shape (`derive_meta` on IMAP and Gmail, `listing::utc` on JMAP and Graph).
- **Moves.** The engine refuses a `Move` meeting a conflict (`conflict-unresolved`). The engine's `Move` is staged in two places, both mail and both through `Stager.mutateMove`: a message moved to another mailbox (`MessageView.file`) and a message deleted into the trash (`MailStager.stageDelete`, from `MessageView.stageDelete`, the reader and a selection alike). Both already catch every exception and show the error dialog a refused marker or removal shows, the engine's sentence under the app's *Error* title; nothing was added. A contact, an event or a task filed elsewhere is an add and a removal, never a `Move`, so no path is new there: their removals over a conflict are refused as draft-10 left them.
- **Stand-ins.** `FileStore.recordAttachments` takes the mailbox of the message and records nothing when another row of the account holds another body under its key, a message whose summary does not agree with its own on subject, date and sender (the app's own `key_of_another_message`). The app records at each open and each download, not only when a body enters, so the rule also holds a first message opened again after a second one arrived: its stand-ins stand, and a reference unlinked by hand does not come back then.
- **The reader's walk.** The reader listed attachments by a walk of its own over mime-rs, which read an entity's fields from their last occurrence and kept the line break ending an unclosed multipart. The list, and the mark shown with it, now come from io-pimdir's `summary::mail::attachments::parts`, and a part is opened from the message parsed after `first_occurrences`, without the line break the message ends with where its own multipart is unclosed, and with no entity bound. Run over every case of summaries.json stating its attachments, the earlier reader disagreed on six fixtures, the two new ones and four older ones: a file sent with no disposition was not listed while its paperclip showed (mail-bare-file, mail-bare-parts), the 1,000 stand-in bound was not kept (mail-many-attachments), and a part past mime-rs's 10,000 entities could not be opened (mail-many-parts). All six agree now.
- **The index.** The extraction is io-pimdir's `PimdirExtract`, driven by its refresh, so both Annex A.1 rules came with the crate. The query client parses with io-pimdir and answers hits alone: it computes no coverage and no completeness, so the rules on a `bodiless` item, on `thread:` and `has:attachment` reading a body, and on a group read through bind nothing here. `subject:a@b` with a wildcard is the parser's.
- **Conformance**: `storage-draft-11 owner (own statement text), sync-draft-11 engine`.

Tests. Left, and for the owner:

- **Device checks.** A build over a draft-10 store opens on the reinstall screen and writes nothing; a fresh install creates the store and the index on the platform SQLite of an Android 8 device, the `CHECK`'s `GLOB` and `unconfirm_action`'s fork included (the floor build ran them on the host alone); a send cut after the final dot reads *May have been sent* and *Send again* puts it back; a message carrying a file with no disposition lists it, and it opens and saves.
- The refusal of a move over a conflict reads as the engine's sentence in the error dialog, as every refused write of a message does. What the reader should say, and whether a conflicted message should be shown as one, is undecided, as for the screens the draft-10 follow left.
- A reader showing the second of two messages reusing one `Message-ID` lists the first one's stand-ins and opens each by its section from the body it shows (STORAGE §14.3 says so, pimdir's O10 left it open): a section the second message lacks fails to open.
- The stand-in rule is checked at each open and download where io-pimdir checks it when a body enters; the two differ only in the case above.
- The reader's display parse keeps mime-rs's 10,000 entity bound; only opening a part parses unbounded, as io-pimdir's extraction does on every stored body.
- A header field stated twice (`Subject`, `From`, `Date`) is read from its first occurrence by mime-rs already; nothing moved there.
- mime-rs 0.1.0 reads an entity's fields from their last occurrence: the pre-pass goes once it reads the first.
- Not started, as scoped: a JVM runner of the sync vectors (A2), coverage and completeness in the query client, the locks.

Capabilities moved: offline-store (the draft, the queue's prefix, a message's date, a part key, the reader's stand-ins), mail (A send that may have gone waits for the user, A message is deleted into the account's trash), conflicts (A move settles no conflict).
