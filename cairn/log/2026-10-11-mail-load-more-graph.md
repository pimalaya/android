# Load more on Microsoft Graph

Capabilities moved: mail (added: a Graph step lists the account by date, Graph bodies come twenty to a batch; modified: a Graph mailbox keeps one unfiltered delta link). Live check and device test pending, so the change stays active.

**Why.** docs/mail-window-files-references-plan.md B.13, Graph bullet: a Graph account stepped from the store (`WidenStep`), widening one folder at a time, and read each body with its own `$value` GET.

**Step.** `GraphStep` (`MailStep.of` for a Graph account) sends the step request every backend shares (`MailEngine.stepRequest`: `{count, held, frontier, bound, anchors}`, the frontier the folder's own over one folder alone) plus `mailboxes` through `MailEngine.stepped(mailbox, request, dir)` and `PimalayaClient.mailStep(session, mailbox, request, dir)`, the one entry point of every step, to `Native.mailStep`, whose Graph branch (rust/src/ffi/mail.rs `graph_step`) runs `graph_mail::step`: `/me/messages` selecting `id,sentDateTime,receivedDateTime,parentFolderId` with the MAPI size expanded, `sentDateTime le F` (and `ge` the bound), ordered `sentDateTime desc`, `$top` 75; ids held at `F` dropped, messages out of the stepped folders (trash, junk, kept whole, outside the roster) skipped, every message sharing the 50th one's `Date` kept; a full page is followed by a keyset re-query from the oldest `Date` reached (`le`, seen ids dropped), below it (`lt`) once a page of that second was all seen, never `nextLink`; `InefficientFilter` or 504 lists each folder and merges by `Date`. The answer is `{exhausted, items}`, each `{handle, mailboxes, sortKey, received, size, path?}`. `GraphStep` then lists every stepped folder down to the oldest `Date` brought (`MailEngine.reach`, a band round unless the folder's coverage reaches it; the whole bound once exhausted), the step's folders first, and stores the bodies from the step's files (`MailEngine.fetched`, `download`), one that did not land owed (`MailOwed`); the frontier skips imports as Gmail's does.

**Bodies.** `graph_mail::read_sources`: `$batch` calls of up to 20 `$value` GETs and `BATCH_BYTES` (4 MB) declared, one at a time; a message past `ALONE_BYTES` (1 MB), of no stated size, or whose batched answer is not base64 (`Inner::Undecoded`) is read alone by a plain GET; an item throttled inside a call waits its `Retry-After` (the longest of the call's, bounded by `WAIT_BUDGET`) once, then goes at the head of the next call; one throttled twice, gone or failing otherwise is left out of the answer, an expired token failing the read. The step reads its own bodies with its listing's sizes, a held message's never; `fetchMessageSources` on Graph reads the sizes first, 20 ids to a `$batch`, then batches the same way. io-msgraph pinned at 2ae2637 (`MsgraphBatchResponse::bytes`, `retry_after`).

**Band.** `Band.skip` and `Ceiling.skip` went: a page of one second's messages all listed already goes on below that second, the delta's first pass naming the rest.

**Tests.** Rust (443 green once merged): graph_mail_tests.rs `step` (fifty across folders past the trash by keyset, the next step at the frontier dropping what is held there, a busy second whole, a small account exhausted, a second fuller than a page left below, the per-folder merge equal to the account's listing, a filtered view), the batches (twenty and 4 MB, the large and the unsized alone, a 429 waited once then left out, an undecoded body read alone, gone and failed left out, an expired token), a crowded second in a band. Java (448 green once merged): `GraphStepTest` (a step over one folder below its own frontier, the account's left alone; a first step bands every folder down to its oldest and stores its bodies, the trash left out; the next step below the frontier moves every floor; an exhausted step lists every folder whole; `MailStep.of`).

**Left open.** The live check that a `$value` inside `$batch` is a base64 string (the test secret's pinentry timed out; a body that is not is read alone, so a wrong guess costs requests, not mail). The device test. Graph executes a batch's items concurrently against its 4 concurrent requests per mailbox, so a call of 20 may draw inner 429s, which wait and go once more. io-pimdir's `size_agrees` compares the MAPI size a Graph listing states with a held body's MIME length, so a Graph body is never linked into another copy; the stepped message's own summary takes the MIME length once its body lands, and the step answers it, but a copy listed later still states the MAPI one.
