# Load more: mail steps instead of dates

Capabilities moved: mail (added: mail syncs on Wi-Fi only, an account's mail comes in steps, an account's frontier is where its steps reached, the mail list ends on Load more, a Gmail step is a set difference, bodies cross the bridge as files, arrivals download whatever their date; modified: an HTTP sync rides out throttling, Gmail is paced below its quota, a mailbox is stored whole, a mailbox is listed a chunk at a time, the merged list reaches down to its mailboxes' floor, older mail fills in behind, an account's mailboxes sync side by side, bodies download behind the list, the mail list shows its background download; removed: an account chooses which bodies it keeps offline; intro). The generic contract and Gmail landed; IMAP, Graph and JMAP keep stepping from the store until their own steps land. Device test pending, so the change stays active.

**Why.** docs/mail-window-files-references-plan.md B.13, after the first Gmail device test of the download window (94 s first sync, a 47 s silent fill, a message impossible to open) and the probes of 2026-10-10: Gmail bills `messages.get` 5 units against 15,000 a minute today, its refusals clear within 2 s, and its binding limit is concurrency (batches of 10: no refusal in 2,250 reads; 25: 1.2 %; 50: 19 %). A date-based floor lets one imported message pull a whole mailbox.

**Generic.** `MailStep` (one per backend, `MailStep.of`) takes one step of about 50 messages on the account's session; `RemotePass.stepMail` runs it for the first sync (`MainActivity.firstMail`) and for the footer (`MainActivity.loadMore`), moving the account's frontier back (`MailWindow.frontier`: the oldest `Date` brought, a message dated more than a day before its reception left out), all mail once exhausted over the whole account. `WidenStep` steps IMAP, Graph and JMAP from the store: it widens the mailbox holding the most recent floor until 50 stored rows lie below the frontier (`MailStore.datesBelow`), at most 8 widenings a step. The footer is *Load more* over *About 50 more per account* (`MailList.info`), disabled with *Waits for Wi-Fi* off Wi-Fi; *Choose a date*, `moveWindow`, `reachStep`, `MailWindow.next`/`begin`, `MailScope.covering` and the footer's sums went. Wi-Fi only: `MainActivity.wifi` gates the pass's listing (`RemotePass.mailPass(scope, listing)`, the outbox still drained), the first sync, the fill and the body step; `waitForWifi` puts *Waiting for Wi-Fi* in the strip and a default-network callback runs what was owed when an unmetered network returns; `BackgroundJob` lists nothing on a metered network. The 256 KB cap (`MailBodies.CAP`, `fits`, `Host.metered`) and *Downloads on Wi-Fi* went. A delta's members new to the store are noted (`MailBodies.ARRIVALS`, process-lived) and planned after the frontier's bodies, whatever their date.

**Bridge.** `Native.fetchMessageSources(transport, session, mailbox, ids, dir)` writes one file per message and answers `{items: [{id, path, size}]}` (Gmail adds `labels`, `received`); Gmail reads them in batches, every other backend through `read_source` one after another. `MailEngine.fetchBodies` reads through it, files each file in the blob store (`PimdirBlobs.put(hash, file)`) and answers the engine `{hash, persisted: size}`, which rust/src/offline.rs hands io-pimdir as `PimdirFetchedBody::Persisted`, its `StoreObject` op then carrying no bytes and `PimdirStorage.storeObject` writing the row alone. `Native.mailStep(transport, session, mailbox, {count, held}, dir)` answers `{historyId, exhausted, items}`, each item named as a listing names it with its `mailboxes`, `path`, `size` and `received`; `GmailStep` lands each mailbox's members through `MailEngine.land` (a round closed on the history id where the mailbox holds nothing, else a delta) and stores their bodies from the step's files (`MailEngine.fetched`), never read again.

**Gmail.** `gmail_sync::step` takes the history id, then walks `messages.list` (no labels, spam and trash out, 500 a page, or one label for a filtered view) from the top, keeping ids not in `held` until the count. `gmail::read_sources` reads them `minimal` then `raw` through `read_all`: batches of 10 (`BATCH`, was 25), raw batches cut at about 8 MB of `sizeEstimate`, a message past 5 MB alone, one batch at a time behind the account's gate; an inner concurrency or missing answer goes back at the head of the next batch after 1 to 2 s, a minute-quota one after `minute_pause` (2 s, a second more each time, 5 s at most), a daily one stops, six tries per message. `gmail::quota` holds every unit and rate (`MESSAGES_GET` 20, 6,000 a minute, 1,000 a second, a burst of 200), with the measured 5 and 15,000 noted; throttle's Gmail minute refusal now waits `minute_pause` and no longer exhausts the account's budget (`gmail_quota_refused`, `MinuteBudget::exhaust` went). The fill skips Gmail (`edges` filter), its mailboxes bring no coverage floor (`MailStore.floorOf`), `GMAIL_FILL_CHUNK`, `accountFloor`, the floor walk and the batch-past-the-floor stop went. History replay stays; a round (expired history, a new label) lists the label's ids since the account's oldest reception (`MailWindow.reach`, sent as `MailRequest.received`) named by the UNREAD and STARRED ids, and `MailEngine.steppedIn` reads by metadata only the members held in another mailbox or listed ahead of every held message.

**Tests.** Rust (395 green): gmail_sync_tests.rs (a step takes the ids not held whatever their dates, exhausted, spam and trash never stepped and a label view steps its label, a first step reads fifty once into every label, an expired history named by markers), gmail_tests.rs (batches of ten with refusals re-queued, raw batches by bytes and lone large messages, minute back-off and daily stop, inner answer classification, raw read fields), throttle (a Gmail minute refusal backs off 2 then 3 s). Java (432 green): `MailStepTest`, `MailWindowTest` frontier and `more`, `MailChunksTest` step from the store, `MailBodiesTest` Wi-Fi gate and arrivals, `MailNamingTest` Gmail round naming; tests of the removed footer, cap and Gmail floor dropped.

**Left open.** The device test. IMAP, Graph and JMAP steps and batching (B.13 per backend). Junk and trash listed on demand when opened. No size on the footer's line: no backend knows a step's size before it runs. A step over one label moves the account's frontier, so the other labels may show a stretch of days they do not hold yet until their own step. `ARRIVALS` is process-lived: an arrival dated below the frontier left over a restart stays a summary until opened. The `mail-download-window` delta still describes the date footer, the cap and the window's first value; its fold takes this change's wording.
